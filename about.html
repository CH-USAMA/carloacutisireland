A life

Fifteen

1991 · 2006

He was an ordinary boy from Milan who catalogued the Eucharistic miracles of the world onto a website, and died before he finished school.

1991London

Carlo Acutis was born on the third of May 1991, in London, to Antonia and Andrea Acutis. He moved to Italy shortly after his birth and grew up in Milan.

MilanA childhood

His parents were wealthy business people who owned an insurance company. They were not religious.

He was an extremely good-natured child, and as a young boy he would often ask his parents deep questions they couldn’t answer. He loved to go into churches to say hello to Jesus and Mary, and would often ask his parents to bring him. He started to get them to question the meaning behind their lives, and slowly he brought them back to their faith again.

OrdinaryAn ordinary boy

Carlo was very ordinary growing up. He loved football, playing music and video games — although he would only allow himself one hour of video games a week, because he felt they were so addictive.

Sadness is the gaze turned towards oneself, happiness is the gaze turned towards God.Carlo Acutis

His friends describe him as a normal guy who wasn’t perfect, but who stood out because of his positive outlook and his good example in everyday life — often helping the bullied, the disabled, the homeless, the migrants.

Everyone is born as an original but many die as photocopies.
Carlo Acutis

The EucharistHis highway to heaven

Always to be united with Jesus, this is my programme of life.Carlo Acutis

Carlo had a great love for God, and aimed to stay close to Jesus and Mary in his life. He loved to pray and to attend mass, and he really believed in the power of the Eucharist — which he called his highway to heaven.

The more we receive the Eucharist, the more we will become like Jesus, so that on this Earth we will have a foretaste of Heaven.Carlo Acutis

He prayed the Rosary daily. Of Mary he said simply that she was the only woman in his life.

The webWhat he built

Carlo couldn’t understand why so many people would attend pop concerts but then never go into a church to see the creator of the universe.

Why do people worry so much about the beauty of their own body, and not the beauty of their soul?Carlo Acutis

So he did the thing he was good at. He taught himself to build websites, and made one documenting Eucharistic miracles from around the world — gathering them, place by place, into a single catalogue that anyone could read. It is still online. The exhibition drawn from it has since travelled the world.

The end

Carlo always believed he would die young, and he tried to live life to the full and not waste it.

When he was fifteen he fell ill with galloping leukemia, and died within the week. He was in great pain. He told the doctors:

“There are others who suffer more than me.”

Monza12 October 2006

2020Assisi

He was beatified in Assisi on the tenth of October 2020, the first millennial to be raised to the altars. His body rests in the Sanctuary of the Renunciation there, where pilgrims come to see him.

2025Rome

He was canonised on the seventh of September 2025, on the parvis of St Peter’s in Rome, together with Pier Giorgio Frassati. His parents Andrea and Antonia were there, with his brother and sister, Michele and Francesca.

You too can become a saint. You just have to want it with all your heart.
Carlo Acutis

And then, Ireland

In January 2023 Brenda Cleary visited his tomb on a day trip to Assisi from Rome. She had never heard of him. She came home determined, and with Archbishop Eamon Martin and Archbishop Domenico Sorrentino she set out to bring his primary relic to every diocese on the island of Ireland. Thousands have since gathered to meet him.
